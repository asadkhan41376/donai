The Gem of Passion and Power

Introduction to Rubies

Rubies, the vibrant red gemstones of the corundum family, are celebrated as the July birthstone and one of the most coveted traditional jewelry stones. Known for their exceptional durability and vivid red hues, rubies have captivated hearts for centuries, symbolizing wealth, power, and protection. At Donai Gems, we offer a curated selection of rubies, showcasing their fiery brilliance and rich history for jewelry enthusiasts and collectors.

The Value of Rubies

Key Factors Affecting Ruby Value

  • Color : The most critical factor, with the finest rubies displaying a vivid, slightly purplish red known as “pigeon’s blood red.” This medium to medium-dark tone with high saturation commands premium prices, with top-quality stones exceeding $1,000,000 per carat.

  • Size : Larger rubies are exponentially more valuable due to their rarity. Even small gem-quality rubies (1–3 carats) command high prices compared to sapphires, starting at around $5,500 per carat for a 1.52-carat stone, depending on clarity.

  • Clarity : Rubies with fewer inclusions are more valuable, though some inclusions, like silk in Myanmar rubies, enhance their appeal by contributing to fluorescence.

  • Origin : Myanmar rubies, particularly from Mogok, are the most prized for their “pigeon’s blood” color and fluorescence, fetching the highest prices. Other sources like Mozambique and Sri Lanka also produce high-quality stones.

  • Treatments : Over 90% of rubies are heat-treated to enhance color and clarity, a widely accepted practice that minimally impacts value. However, treatments like diffusion or glass-filling significantly reduce value.

Pricing Insights

  • Myanmar Rubies : Mogok rubies with “pigeon’s blood” color can exceed $1,000,000 per carat for large, untreated stones. Möng Hsu rubies, with dark cores, are less expensive but often heat-treated.

  • Mozambique Rubies : Since 2009, Montepuez has produced rubies ranging from Myanmar-like to Thai-like colors, with untreated stones commanding high prices.

  • Sri Lankan Rubies : Lighter, often pinkish or purplish, these are brilliant and can be magnificent in larger sizes (5+ carats).

  • Thai/Cambodian Rubies : High-clarity but garnet-like due to high iron content, less valuable than Myanmar stones.

  • Other Sources : Madagascar, Vietnam, Afghanistan, and Nepal produce quality rubies, with varying price points based on color and clarity.

Ruby Characteristics

What Makes a Ruby?

Rubies are gem-quality red corundum, colored by trace amounts of chromium. The color ranges from pinkish reds to purplish or brownish reds, with the finest stones exhibiting a medium to medium-dark red tone. Lighter red corundum is classified as pink sapphire, though the boundary is debated among gemologists. At Donai Gems, we ensure our rubies meet the highest standards of red coloration.

Inclusions and Unique Features

  • Silk Inclusions : Myanmar rubies often contain fine rutile silk, contributing to their intense fluorescence and glowing appearance.

  • Asterism : Star rubies, cut as cabochons, display a six-rayed star effect due to precisely arranged rutile inclusions. Mogok produces the world’s finest star rubies.

  • Color Zoning : Common in Mogok rubies, with rich red patches or swirls, enhancing their visual appeal.

  • Ruby-in-Zoisite : A unique lapidary material (anyolite) with opaque ruby in chrome-rich green zoisite and black hornblende, often carved into intricate designs.

Fluorescence

Rubies fluoresce under ultraviolet (UV) light, aiding origin identification:

  • Myanmar Rubies : Intense red fluorescence, visible even in sunlight, creating a glowing effect.

  • Sri Lankan Rubies : Strong fluorescence, often with pinkish or purplish hues.

  • Thai/Cambodian Rubies : Lack fluorescence due to high iron content, appearing garnet-like.

  • Other Sources : Mozambique and Afghan rubies show strong fluorescence, while Vietnamese rubies may have blue patches.

Absorption Spectrum

Rubies exhibit a characteristic chromium spectrum with lines at 6942/6928 (doublet) in the red, a broad band at 5500, and weak lines in the blue-green. This helps distinguish natural from synthetic rubies, with synthetics sometimes showing additional lines (e.g., 4740 in flux-grown stones).

Major Sources of Rubies

Rubies are mined globally, with key sources including:

  • Myanmar : The premier source, with Mogok producing “pigeon’s blood” rubies for over 1,000 years. Möng Hsu is a newer, abundant source with darker, often treated stones. Nanyazeik is an emerging deposit.

  • Mozambique : Montepuez, since 2009, yields rubies rivaling Myanmar’s, available in various sizes and treatments.

  • Sri Lanka : Known for brilliant, lighter-toned rubies, often with strong fluorescence and occasional star effects.

  • Thailand/Cambodia : High-clarity but garnet-like due to high iron, less valuable, with negligible production today.

  • Madagascar : Vatomandry and Andilamena produce lighter, Myanmar-like and darker rubies, respectively, often heat-treated.

  • Vietnam : VLuc Yen and other deposits yield rubies approaching Mogok quality, though often pinker and less clear.

  • Afghanistan : Jegdalek produces rare, high-quality rubies with strong fluorescence and occasional blue patches.

  • Nepal : A newer source with small, color-zoned crystals (5 carats or less).

Synthetic Rubies and Simulants

Synthetic Rubies

Synthetic rubies, produced since the late 19th century via flame fusion (Verneuil process), Czochralski, flux, or hydrothermal methods, mimic natural stones closely. They often have curved striae and different fluorescence (e.g., weak red in SW for flame-fusion stones). At Donai Gems, we use advanced gemological testing to distinguish synthetics, ensuring authenticity.

Simulants

Simulants like red spinel, garnet, glass, and cubic zirconia may mimic rubies but differ in hardness and optical properties. Misleading trade names like “Serengeti Rubies” or “Cape Rubies” may be used, but our gemologists ensure accurate identification.

Ruby Enhancements

Most rubies are heat-treated to enhance color and dissolve silk inclusions, an accepted practice that minimally affects value if disclosed. Less accepted treatments, like diffusion or glass-filling, significantly reduce value. At Donai Gems, we provide full transparency about all treatments.

Notable Rubies

Rubies are renowned for their size and historical significance

  • Rosser Reeves Star Ruby : 138.7 carats, Smithsonian Institution.

  • De Long Star Ruby : 100 carats, American Museum of Natural History.

  • Cathedral of St. Guy Ruby : 250 carats, Prague.

  • Chhatrapati Manik Ruby : A historic gem of notable size.

  • Peace Ruby: : 43 carats, a significant specimen.

Caring for Your Ruby Jewelry

Rubies’ exceptional hardness (9 on the Mohs scale) and lack of cleavage make them ideal for everyday jewelry, including rings and engagement rings:

  • Cleaning : For rubies free of significant inclusions or fractures, mechanical cleaning (ultrasonic or steam) is safe. For heavily included stones, use warm, soapy water and a soft brush to avoid damage.

  • Storage : Store rubies separately to prevent scratching softer gems. Protective settings, like bezels, enhance durability in rings.

  • Care Tips : Avoid harsh chemicals and extreme heat, especially for treated stones, to maintain their appearance.
